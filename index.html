Why it's built this way

  1. The Hindi alphabet is already a sound chart. क-ख-ग-घ-ङ are all made at the same spot in the mouth; each row moves forward from throat to lips, and each column changes one thing — a puff of air (महाप्राण) or a throat hum (घोष). The अक्षर tab shows exactly this, with a mouth diagram.
  2. Ear before mouth. Children who mostly speak English often can't hear क/ख, त/ट or कि/की apart, so they can't say them either. The सुनो game and the गायब अक्षर game drill exactly these contrasts.
  3. Say it, get feedback. The बोलो tab listens through the microphone, compares what it heard with the target word, marks the akshara that went wrong, and names the likely cause — no puff, tongue not curled, vowel too short — with the fix.
  4. Blend by akshara. Words are split into aksharas (कु·त्ता) so reading becomes sound-by-sound blending, not guessing.
  5. Spaced repetition. Known words return after 1, 3, 7, then 14 days; missed words return the same day.

About the speaking check

It uses the browser's own speech service (Google in Chrome and Edge, Apple on iPhone and iPad), so it needs internet, and the recording is processed by that service. The service tends to "hear" the nearest real Hindi word, so it can miss small slips and is more forgiving than a teacher. Treat it as a coach; you're still the best judge. The tissue test (ख flutters a tissue, क doesn't) and hand-on-throat test (ग buzzes, क doesn't) settle most doubts.

Outside the app

Use Hindi for everyday things at meals ("पानी दो", "रोटी चाहिए"), let him teach you a word he learnt today, and read a short Hindi picture book together at bedtime. Ten to fifteen minutes a day beats an hour on Sunday.

Letter + matra = syllable

Pick a consonant. Each box shows it joined with one vowel sign. Tap to hear, or play the whole row.

Watch out for छोटी इ (ि): it is written before the letter but said after it. कि = क + इ. Hold बड़ी मात्रा (ा ी ू) about twice as long as the short one.
